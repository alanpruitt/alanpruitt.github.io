### Anatomy of the Lineage Gap: Why Generations 1 and 2 Stalled Before the Classroom

When I audited eight years of open-source commits, public-sector AI pilots, and academic preprints for **[Webcognita LLC Technical Report `S-A-R-v2.2-ORIGIN`](https://alanpruitt.com/curriculum-as-code/)**, a clear three-generation arc emerged—along with the exact reasons the first two waves stalled before reaching everyday higher education:

1. **Generation 1 (2018–2020) — Ephemeral Kubernetes Infrastructure (`NRE Labs` & Project Antidote):** The earliest explicit industrial formulation of *"Curriculum-as-Code"* was coined in **October 2018** by **Matt Oswalt** and cloud-native engineers at **Network Reliability Engineering (`NRE`) Labs** alongside **Project Antidote** ([`github.com/nre-learning/nrelabs-curriculum`](https://github.com/nre-learning/nrelabs-curriculum)). Watching network engineering shift from manual router CLI edits to Infrastructure-as-Code (`IaC`), they stored lesson definitions and verification scripts in Git so Kubernetes could spin up live browser sandboxes on demand. Generation 1 proved pedagogy could be peer-reviewed via pull requests—but spinning up heavyweight virtual machines for every student priced general higher education out of the room.
2. **Generation 2 (2021–2025) — Plain-Text SSoTs & Flat Document Compilers:** A second wave stripped away Kubernetes overhead by adapting Docs-as-Code (`Markdown`, `YAML`, `LaTeX`, `Pandoc`, and GitHub Actions) to static documents. In **August 2021**—more than a year before ChatGPT's public release, during my 15-year executive tenure directing regional planning for the **Western Arizona Economic Development District (`WAEDD`, 2007–2022)**—I engineered a multi-county **Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy (`CEDS`)** manual using an early-stage open-source AI Co-Pilot model from a GitHub repository, earning the national **2021 NADO Aliceann Wohlbruck Impact Award**. Between 2023 and August 2026, university STEM faculty—including Paiva's *Curriculum-as-Code* (`arXiv:2608.07364`)—formalized `YAML` + `LaTeX/Beamer` pipelines to keep syllabi and lecture slides synchronized.

Yet stopping at Generation 2 leaves four structural fault lines wide open:

- **Flat Files Without Pedagogical Topology:** Compiling `YAML` to `Beamer` PDF slides creates zero machine-readable graph linking a clinical safety rule to its **Course Learning Outcome (`CLO`)**, lab rubric, and federal guideline.
- **Downstream Accessibility Debt:** Static PDF and Markdown compilers do not remediate live Learning Management System (`LMS`) HTML sanitizers or guarantee `0 Open Issues` under **ADA Title II (`28 CFR Part 35`)**.
- **Unverified Chatbot Copy-Pasting:** Handing students static notes alongside commercial cloud chatbots invites passive offloading and dangerous clinical hallucinations.
- **Cloud FERPA Exposure & Textbook Paywalls:** Commercial `$180` publisher access codes and `$20/month` cloud AI subscriptions exclude working-class students before Week 1 begins.

### Engineering Generation 3: `S-A-R v2.2` in a Borderland HSI

Context dictates architecture. Drawing on my experience as an **Adjunct Faculty member of Exercise Science & Wellness at Arizona Western College**, a pro bono **Generative AI Strategist (`DCC`) at The University of Arizona (School of Nutritional Sciences and Wellness — Yuma Campus)**, and **Founder & Principal Consultant of Webcognita LLC (`SDVOSB / VOSB`)**, I independently engineered **Generation 3 (`S-A-R v2.2` — Scalable / Auditable / Repeatable)** for the students directly in front of me.

Arizona Western College is a rural borderland **Hispanic-Serving Institution (`HSI`)** where **77% of students are Latino** (`3.08x` the 25% federal Title V HSI threshold) and **62% are first-generation college students** (*"At AWC, 77% of students are Latino, and 62% are first-generation students. We serve this beautiful community, but challenge is everywhere,"* AWC President Dr. Reetika Dhawan, as quoted in *The Yuma Sun*, November 11, 2026).
