> **In Plain English:** Before you read the details below, here is the short version: **Dual-horizon terminology governance links new federal "Super Intelligence (SI)" rules with existing "Artificial Intelligence (AI)" laws in one searchable system.** When federal agencies change official vocabulary overnight, keyword-only databases miss critical grants, contracts, and safety rules. Pairing exact keyword search with semantic meaning keeps community colleges, workforce boards, and small businesses visible and compliant.

When **Executive Order 14434** (*91 FR 63129*, signed September 29, 2026) directed federal Executive Branch departments and agencies—now visibly rolling out across the FDA, NIH, Department of Justice, and Department of State—to adopt **"Super Intelligence" ("SI")** in place of **"Artificial Intelligence" ("AI")** across non-statutory correspondence, public websites, requests for information (`RFIs`), and program guidance, most organizations treated it as a cosmetic find-and-replace exercise.

A hasty find-and-replace, however, breaks statutory compliance just as badly as ignoring the order altogether. **Executive Order 14434** explicitly anchors technical scope back to **`15 U.S.C. 9401(3)`**—where Congress codified the statutory definition of *"artificial intelligence"*—and preserves existing statutory and regulatory citations such as **U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) ETA Advisory TEN No. 07-25** and **NIST AI RMF 1.0**.

Whenever federal nomenclature bifurcates between executive policy and statutory code, I test my own systems against a straightforward counterfactual question:

**What if I didn't bridge the nomenclature?**

What if I left my curriculum repositories, institutional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (`RAG`) indexes, and federal contracting profiles locked solely to legacy `"AI"` strings—or blindly scrubbed `"Artificial Intelligence"` out of statutory citations where federal law requires it?

In high-compliance education, clinical training, and federal procurement, vocabulary drift is architecture drift.

### Three Structural Failures When Federal Vernacular Shifts Overnight

1. **Lexical Blindness in Keyword-Only Portals & VMS Filters:** Traditional Boolean filters—whether inside `SAM.gov` pre-award notices or extended-workforce marketplaces like **MBO Partners / Beeline**—match literal character strings. Pure lexical search misses `SI` notices when a profile only lists `AI`, while pure vector search blurs exact statutory citations like `15 U.S.C. 9401(3)` or `28 CFR Part 35`.
2. **Statutory Corruption & Defense Acronym Collision (`SI` vs. `TS/SCI`):** Having served in **U.S. Marine Corps and U.S. Army Counterintelligence** holding a Defense Investigative Service **Top Secret / Sensitive Compartmented Information (`TS/SCI — Special Intelligence / TK`)** clearance, I know that in defense security channels `SI/TK` historically denoted *Special Intelligence*—not Executive Branch *Super Intelligence*. Without explicit schema disambiguation, parsers conflate military clearances with generative computing architectures.
3. **Cloud Wrapper Lock-In & Retrofitted Accessibility (`ADA Title II` / `Section 508`):** Scrambling to rebrand around `SI` via commercial SaaS wrappers leaks `FERPA`, `HIPAA`, or `FCI` payloads off-premise while failing mandatory **ADA Title II (`28 CFR Part 35`)**, **Section 508**, and **WCAG 2.2 AA (`9:1` contrast)** gates.
