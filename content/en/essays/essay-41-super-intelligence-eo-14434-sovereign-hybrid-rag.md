When **Executive Order 14434** (*91 FR 63129*, signed September 29, 2026) directed federal Executive Branch departments and agencies—now visibly rolling out across the FDA, NIH, Department of Justice, and Department of State—to adopt **"Super Intelligence" ("SI")** in place of **"Artificial Intelligence" ("AI")** across non-statutory correspondence, public websites, requests for information (`RFIs`), and program guidance, most organizations treated it as a cosmetic find-and-replace exercise.

A hasty find-and-replace, however, breaks statutory compliance just as badly as ignoring the order altogether. **Executive Order 14434** explicitly anchors technical scope back to **`15 U.S.C. 9401(3)`**—where Congress codified the statutory definition of *"artificial intelligence"*—and preserves existing statutory and regulatory citations such as **U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) ETA Advisory TEN No. 07-25** and **NIST AI RMF 1.0**.

Whenever federal nomenclature bifurcates between executive policy and statutory code, I test my own systems against a straightforward counterfactual question:

## 1. Pattern: Three Structural Failures When Federal Vernacular Shifts Overnight

Across community colleges, **Hispanic-Serving Institutions (HSIs)**, tribal nations, and federal/enterprise vendor management systems (`VMS`), a sudden split between **`Super Intelligence (SI)`** and **`Artificial Intelligence (AI)`** triggers three immediate operational failures:

1. **Lexical Blindness in Keyword-Only Search & Procurement Portals:**
   Traditional Boolean keyword filters—whether inside `SAM.gov` pre-award notices, agency forecast spreadsheets, or enterprise extended-workforce marketplaces such as **MBO Partners / Beeline**—match literal character tokens. If a federal program officer issues a `Sources Sought` notice for *"Super Intelligence (`SI`) Workforce Governance"* while an institution's capability index only contains *"Artificial Intelligence (`AI`) Literacy,"* a pure lexical search returns zero hits. Conversely, a pure vector-only search blurs exact statutory citations like `15 U.S.C. 9401(3)`, `28 CFR Part 35`, or `FAR 52.204-21`.
2. **Statutory Citation Corruption & Defense Acronym Collision (`SI` vs. `TS/SCI`):**
   Naive global text replacement corrupts codified federal titles (such as **U.S. DOL ETA TEN No. 07-25: *Artificial Intelligence Literacy Framework***) and creates an immediate collision with defense counterintelligence terminology. Having served in **U.S. Marine Corps and U.S. Army Counterintelligence** holding a Defense Investigative Service **Top Secret / Sensitive Compartmented Information (`TS/SCI`)** clearance, I know firsthand that inside defense security channels, legacy `SI/TK` stood for *Special Intelligence / Talent Keyhole*—not Executive Branch *Super Intelligence*. Without explicit schema disambiguation, automated resume parsers and LLM answer engines conflate compartmented intelligence clearances with generative computing architectures.
3. **Cloud Wrapper Lock-In & Retrofitted Accessibility (`ADA Title II` / `Section 508`):**
   Organizations scrambling to rebrand around `SI` routinely purchase commercial cloud chatbot wrappers that route sensitive student (`FERPA`), clinical (`HIPAA`), or Federal Contract Information (`FCI`) payloads off-premise while failing mandatory **ADA Title II (`28 CFR Part 35`)**, **Section 508**, and **WCAG 2.2 AA** contrast and screen-reader gates.
