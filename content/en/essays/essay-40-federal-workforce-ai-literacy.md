When the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) Employment and Training Administration (ETA) issued **Training and Employment Notice (TEN) No. 07-25** (*Artificial Intelligence Literacy Framework*, February 13, 2026), most higher education and workforce programs treated it like another static policy PDF.

A typical compliance response would have been simple: paste a two-sentence "AI Policy" paragraph into a course syllabus, link a generic slide deck on prompt writing, and check the administrative box.

Every time I encounter a federal workforce standard, however, I test my own engineering against the same counterfactual question:

**What if I didn't operationalize the framework?**

What if I left **U.S. DOL ETA Advisory TEN No. 07-25** trapped inside a policy memo—while the first-generation community college students I teach along the **Yuma, AZ / Imperial Valley, CA / San Luis Río Colorado** borderland graduated into an AI-reshaped clinical and allied-health workforce knowing only how to copy-paste unverified chatbot outputs?

Policy memos do not train problem framers. Executable architecture does.

## Anatomy of the Policy-to-Classroom Gap

Across community colleges, **Hispanic-Serving Institutions (HSIs)**, and state workforce boards, federal AI literacy guidance routinely stalls at three structural bottlenecks:

1. **Syllabus Theater vs. Daily Cognitive Habit:**
   Listing AI literacy outcomes on page 9 of a syllabus changes nothing if weekly assignments still reward passive recall or allow unverified generative copy-paste.
2. **Cloud Vendor Lock-In & FERPA Exposure:**
   Routing student coursework through commercial SaaS wrappers exposes student records (`PII`) to external cloud telemetry, imposes recurring seat-license fees on working families, and breaks portability across Learning Management Systems.
3. **Retrofitted Accessibility & Linguistic Exclusion:**
   Under federal **ADA Title II (`28 CFR Part 35`)** and Section 508 mandates, workforce AI tools must be born accessible. Yet commercial widgets routinely fail screen-reader semantics, ship low-contrast `4.5:1` UI chrome under `100,000+ lux` desert sunlight, and ignore authentic regional **Norteño-Spanish** clinical phrasing.

### Part A: Operationalizing the 5 Foundational AI Content Areas

Rather than teaching AI in an isolated computer-science elective, I bound all **5 Foundational Content Areas** of **TEN No. 07-25** into **The Centaur Protocol** (*Student as Clinical Architect / Pilot; AI as Probabilistic Calculator*) and my mandatory **Four-Part Submission Standard**:

1. **Content Area 1 — Understand AI Principles (`The Centaur Protocol`):**
   Every module grounds students in why Large Language Models are probabilistic token predictors—never clinical authorities—requiring deterministic human framing (`Pattern / Rule / Solve`).
2. **Content Area 2 — Explore AI Uses (`Zero-Login Clinical Edge Simulators`):**
   Students test occupational cases across my open-access web workbenches (`exwtoolkit.com` 9-Box Biometrics & Live NWS `KNYL` Yuma Heat-Stress Engine and `paga2018.dev` Interactive Guidelines PWA) without accounts, paywalls, or tracking cookies.
3. **Content Area 3 — Direct AI Effectively (`Part 1: P-T-C-F Prompt Engineering`):**
   Students construct structured **Persona, Task, Context, and Format (`P-T-C-F`)** prompts encapsulating de-identified clinical biometrics inside strict triple-hash (`###`) structural delimiters.
4. **Content Area 4 — Evaluate Outputs (`Part 2: 150-Word Federal SSoT Audit`):**
   Students audit AI-generated calculations line-by-line against non-negotiable **Federal Single Sources of Truth** (**PAGA 2018 2nd Ed.**, **2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans ["Real Food" Focus]**, **ACSM 12th Ed.**, and the **NASM OPT Model**).
5. **Content Area 5 — Use AI Responsibly (`Part 3: 150-Word Active Verification & FERPA Gate`):**
   Every Lab and Coaching Sprint requires a **150-word verbatim defense in the student's own human voice** justifying Yuma heat-safety gates and Registered Dietitian / Physician scope-of-practice referrals, backed by automated `0-PII` Git pre-commit linters.
