Broken links are just the visible damage. The real cost is configuration drift.

In higher education and enterprise training, it’s common practice to pull external feeds and dynamic resources directly into Learning Management Systems (LMS). But without an upstream architecture governing those assets, you aren’t just risking 404 errors—you’re silently degrading version control, audit trails, and accessibility compliance.

When an external link dies or an unversioned dependency shifts, the fix is rarely simple. Instructional teams are left manually patching broken paths across dozens of course shells and modules.

The solution isn't better manual monitoring. It’s treating instructional content like version-controlled software.

In **Essay 18: The Illusion of the Feed**, I break down:
• Why dynamic feed dependencies create structural vulnerabilities in LMS environments
• How upstream Single Source of Truth (SSoT) architecture stops link rot before deployment
• The role of continuous integration (CI) and automated linting in maintaining Title II/WCAG compliance
• Why the "update once, deploy everywhere" model is essential for scalable curriculum design

If you're managing complex digital learning ecosystems, relying on unanchored feeds is an architectural debt you eventually have to pay back.
